## What the application must say

### 1. § 123 SGB XI model project, with a Kommune or Wohlfahrtsverband as applicant

The applicant is `[Kommune oder Träger]` in `[Land]`, with Land or Kommune co-funding (§ 123). Anspruch is one component of the project. ZAPF@DigitAl in Schleswig-Holstein is the verified precedent for a funded regional project on this route. The application should say:

- **The problem, in the statute's own terms.** Families have a right to Pflegeberatung (§ 7a) and to an appointment within two weeks of a first application (§ 7b). Anspruch prepares them for that appointment and for the Begutachtung.
- **What the tool does.** Estimates the likely Pflegegrad from 12 to 20 questions in a typical intake, compares the entitlement with what the household receives, lists the steps with their statute, and produces a PDF in German or English with the answers given. Interface in six languages.
- **Why figures can be trusted.** Every amount and threshold carries its statutory source and the date it was last checked (`lib/rules/sources.ts`, all amounts checked 22 Oct 2026). Unsettled results are shown as a range. Conditional benefits are never counted as certain money.
- **Privacy.** Answers stay on the device; no account, no server storing answers; counting is cookieless and never includes an answer or the grade.
- **What it sends people to.** The tool tells families that § 7a advice is free and is their right, so it feeds the counselling the project funds rather than replacing it.
- **How success is measured.** Per partner link, monthly: visits, intakes started, results shown (with the questions band, the result type and the monthly-gap band), PDFs downloaded, interface language. Plus the counsellors' own tally of applications, upgrade requests and objections that followed. No outcome is promised in advance.
- **What is still open.** The PNOG cabinet bill of 30 Sep 2026 is not law; if passed as drafted, most figures change from 1 Jan 2027 and the project budget must include that update. The native-speaker review of four languages is owed. Both go into `[Zeitplan]` and `[Budget]`.

### 2. Prototype Fund, Jahrgang 03 (deadline 30 Nov 2026)

Apply only after Q3, Q4 and Q5 are answered. The deadline leaves about five weeks from this draft. The application should say:

- **The framing the fund asks for.** Anspruch is software that does on the device what usually needs a server: the estimate, the saved session and the PDF are all built in the browser, with no account and no upload. That is the argument for Resilienz or Innovation (Q4).
- **What is open-source and when.** Name the licence chosen (see below) and the repository. The fund reportedly requires open-source publication (to confirm, Q3 and Q5).
- **What the funded months build.** `[Zeitplan]`: for example the native-speaker review, the PNOG update if it passes, and partner pilots. Each item must be a real plan with `[Budget]`.
- **Who applies.** `[Team]`: a natural person or a team of up to four, taxed in Germany (Q3).
- **Evidence it is used.** The same monthly numbers as above, from the deployed site at `[Adresse der Seite]`, and the names of `[Pilotpartner]` that have agreed.

## Licence decision

`README.md` says "Not yet chosen". The scout found these points (section 4 of the scout file):

- **EUPL-1.2.** OSI-certified. Reciprocal. Its compatibility list includes GPL, AGPL, LGPL, EPL and MPL; Apache is not in that list ([EU FAQ](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/eupl/faq), verified).
- **AGPL-3.0.** Copyleft. Third parties may host and self-brand the code free, so revenue rests on hosting, support, updates and statute maintenance rather than on code exclusivity (scout's reasoning, not sourced).
- **Apache-2.0.** Permissive. Partners could fork privately and close the code (scout's reasoning).
- **No open licence.** Keeps the code exclusive for a licensed version, but the Prototype Fund reportedly requires open-source publication (to confirm, Q3).
- **Selling a licensed version under any of the open options.** As sole copyright holder the founder can still dual-license and sell a proprietary or SLA-backed version. This needs sole copyright: no outside contributions without a contributor licence agreement (scout's reasoning, not sourced).
- Which licence is most common for grant-funded German civic tech: not found.

**Recommendation: EUPL-1.2, with sole copyright kept and a contributor licence agreement before any outside contribution.** It is the one option whose OSI certification the scout verified directly, so it should meet an open-source condition such as the Prototype Fund's. It is reciprocal, so a partner cannot take the code private, which protects the privacy promise organisations are buying into. Its compatibility list includes AGPL and GPL, so it does not close the door on combining with that code later. Sole copyright keeps the dual-licence route open for a paid, maintained version for organisations. Decide before 30 Nov 2026 if the Prototype Fund application goes ahead, and have a lawyer confirm the dual-licensing point, which the scout did not source.
